\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\begin{otherlanguage}{english}
In early 2024, a research line was established within the Atmospheric Physics Group to investigate the possible existence of microbial life in liquid veins within ice \cite{mader2006,rohde2007,dani2012}. Its initial stages examined the motion of microorganisms from stagnant water samples collected in Córdoba between 0 °C and 15 °C, with an interest in their activity near the melting point of ice \cite{pedernera2024}. Videos were acquired using a camera coupled to an optical microscope. Studying their trajectories, mean speeds, and possible relationship with temperature motivated the need for individual measurements; four types of microorganisms were distinguished in the initial samples.

Manual trajectory reconstruction using Tracker requires recording positions across numerous frames. To support this analysis, this thesis developed a computer vision system integrating a YOLO11 detector adapted to the available material, BoT-SORT tracking, and position recording by identifier. From these positions, it computes distances, speed, changes in speed, and temporal indicators, applies a rule-based classification of motion patterns, and generates outputs for inspection.

The evaluation includes records from two training runs, controlled tests, and a comparison between one automated trajectory and a partial manual reference for \texttt{primer\_minuto.mp4}, a video identified through confirmation by the author. After aligning the coordinate systems using an initial anchor, the 92 subsequent matched positions had a mean separation of $5.33$ px. Speed comparisons revealed discrepancies requiring a separate assessment of derived quantities. Numerical checks supported the use of actual frame intervals in the examined cases.

The computational contribution lies in integrating and adapting the processing workflow to the material of this experimental research line. Tracking evidence is limited to the studied case; broader evaluation, independent validation of the categories, and measurement of time savings remain pending. The thesis does not evaluate temperature effects on motion. Measurements remain in px, px/s, and px/s$^2$; optical calibration and conversion to physical units belong to a stage outside the scope of this work.
\end{otherlanguage}
